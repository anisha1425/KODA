Chapter One

The Beginning

The morning sun broke through the heavy curtains, casting long, dusty beams across the wooden floorboards. It was a silence so profound that even the motes of dust seemed to hang suspended in time, afraid to disturb the stillness.

Elara sat by the window, her hands clasped tightly around a porcelain teacup that had long since grown cold. She watched the world outside—a tapestry of greens and browns that stretched endlessly towards the horizon. In her mind, she replayed the events of the previous night, each memory sharper than a blade.

"They said it couldn't be done," she whispered to the empty room. Her voice was barely audible, a ghost of a sound that vanished as quickly as it had come. But the words lingered in her mind, heavy and undeniable.

The letter lay on the small oak table beside her. Its seal was broken, the red wax crumbling like dried blood. She didn't need to read it again to know its contents by heart. The invitation was clear, the threat veiled but unmistakable.

She stood up, the chair scraping harshly against the floor. It was time. Delaying would only make the inevitable harder to bear. She walked to the wardrobe, pulling out a heavy woolen coat. The air outside would be biting, and she had a long journey ahead.

...

As she stepped out onto the porch, the wind whipped her hair across her face. The scent of pine and damp earth filled her lungs, grounding her. This was her home, her sanctuary. And she would fight to protect it, no matter the cost.
